How to Prove Field Work Was Completed with GPS Photos & Reports

Learn how to document field visits using GPS route tracking, timestamped photos and professional PDF reports—without stitching together evidence from different apps.

By Track ReporterOctober 26, 2026 · Field work documentation guide

How can you demonstrate that a field worker visited a job site and documented the work performed? A GPS route, location-tagged photographs and a structured report can provide a useful record of the visit.

For inspectors, maintenance teams, field technicians and contractors, documenting work can be almost as important as doing it. Manual timesheets, paper checklists and photos spread across messaging apps make it difficult to reconstruct a site visit.

Track Reporter brings route recording, photo evidence and PDF reporting together in an Android app built around professional field activities.

What is proof of work in field services?

Proof of work is documentation that helps show where, when and how a field activity took place. It is useful when a supervisor or customer needs more context than a written confirmation that a job was completed.

Example: a multi-site inspection

A technician visits several facilities in one day. A record that combines GPS history, photos and timestamps helps link individual stops to the inspection activity and supports a clearer report for the customer.

Good field documentation can help answer practical questions: Which locations were visited? When did the worker arrive? What conditions were photographed? How can the visit be reviewed later?

Why GPS tracking alone is not enough

A GPS tracker can show a route, but a route alone does not establish which task was performed at a stop. Similarly, a photograph without location or time context may be difficult to connect to the correct visit.

For stronger documentation, combine three complementary elements:

⌖

Location

A recorded GPS route documents where the device traveled.

◷

Time

Timestamps establish when recorded events occurred.

▧

Photos

Images provide visual context for inspected conditions or completed tasks.

These records provide supporting evidence, not an absolute guarantee that a task was completed. GPS reception, device settings and documentation practices affect the reliability of any record.

How to document field work in five steps

01

Start recording the GPS route

Begin the recording session when starting field work. Track Reporter records GPS positions and timestamps and presents the route on a map so you can review the visit afterward.

02

Capture photo evidence on site

Photograph inspected areas, equipment or job results. Track Reporter can associate photos with geographic coordinates, times, categories and notes.

03

Continue documenting offline

Route recording does not require continuous mobile data. This is useful at remote installations or sites with poor connectivity. GPS accuracy still depends on reception and the device.

04

Review the route and activity history

Use the map and route playback to review the recorded journey and relate individual stops to photo evidence.

05

Generate a PDF field report

Bring route information and photo evidence into a professional report to share with a customer, supervisor or colleague.
