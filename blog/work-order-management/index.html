What Is a Field Service Work Order?

A field service Work Order is a job assigned to a person or team, such as inspecting a property, checking a construction site or completing a maintenance visit. It helps connect the assignment, responsible worker, progress and completion review in one place.

Track Reporter adds a useful field-work dimension: a worker can record a GPS route and associate that recorded session with the Work Order. A route shows where the device traveled during recording; photos and reports can provide additional context when available.

How Work Order Management Works in Track Reporter

1

Create a Work Order

A fleet administrator creates a job for a field visit or task. Give the Work Order a clear description so the assigned member knows what needs to be done.

2

Assign the Job to a Fleet Member

Select the team member responsible for the assignment. The member can then open their own Work Orders and see the job details.

3

Update Work Order Progress

Use statuses such as Assigned, In Progress and Completed to distinguish pending jobs from active and finished work.

4

Record a GPS Route and Link It to the Job

Record a field visit with the Android app, then select a recorded route to associate with the relevant Work Order. This connects a particular assignment with route history rather than leaving the evidence in a separate list.

5

Review the Completed Work

Fleet administrators can review the Work Order status and the associated recorded route. Where a route includes photos or other session details, those records can help explain the field activity.

Understand Work Order Statuses

Clear statuses make it easier to see which assignments still need attention. Track Reporter uses these Work Order states:

Assigned

A job has been assigned to a fleet member.

In Progress

The assigned job is being worked on.

Completed

The job has been marked as completed.

Rejected

The assigned job has been rejected, with a reason when provided.

Cancelled

The Work Order has been cancelled, with a reason when provided.

Fleet administrators can also reopen a Work Order when additional work or review is needed.

Why Link GPS Routes to Work Orders?

Traditional task lists show whether someone marked a job complete. A linked GPS route adds context about the recorded field visit, including the route and timing. If photos were captured during the session, they can provide visual context for the work.

GPS data is supporting evidence, not automatic proof of every task.

A recorded route indicates device movement during a session. It does not independently verify the quality of an inspection or that every required task was performed. Review the job details and supporting documentation together.

Android Work Orders and the Fleet Web Experience

The Android app supports field activity, Work Orders and GPS route recording. Track Reporter also provides a browser-based fleet experience for managing and reviewing field operations. Available actions can differ between the Android and web interfaces.

Route recording can continue offline in the Android app. Internet access is needed for online fleet features and synchronization; offline route recording should not be confused with offline Work Order synchronization.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I assign Work Orders to fleet members?

Yes. Fleet administrators can create and assign Work Orders to members of their fleet.

Can I attach a previously recorded GPS route to a Work Order?

Yes. Track Reporter supports selecting a recorded route and associating it with a Work Order.

What Work Order statuses are available?

Assigned, In Progress, Completed, Rejected and Cancelled. Administrators can also reopen a Work Order when needed.

Does a GPS route prove that every task was completed?

No. A route documents recorded movement and can support a review, but completion should be assessed alongside the job requirements and other available evidence.

Is Track Reporter a vehicle telematics platform?

No. Track Reporter focuses on field work documentation, Work Orders, GPS routes, photos and shared fleet activity rather than vehicle hardware or engine diagnostics.
